On-device redaction for documents & images

Redact the document,
not just the text.

phi-redact finds names, dates, ID numbers — and faces — inside PDFs and images. It shows you every box it proposes, waits for your review, and only then burns the redaction in. The file never leaves your computer.

No. 1

Found locally

OCR, face detection and the redaction model all run on the device holding the document. A tool for sensitive files cannot itself be a place files are sent.

No. 2

Reviewed by a person

Automatic burn-in exists, but it is not the default. The review screen is the product: accept, reject, peek behind a box, or draw one the model missed.

No. 3

Burned in, not covered

A black rectangle drawn over a PDF is not a redaction — the text survives beneath it. phi-redact removes the content and strips the metadata.

Python, TypeScript, Rust and Swift builds of the engine produce byte-identical output, verified by SHA-256 digests over canonical results — at every document length tested, 100 to 50,000 characters. Known exception, disclosed here rather than buried: scored digests at 2,000+ characters can differ in the fourth decimal of a span score. Redacted text: identical.

A note on what this is not. phi-redact is a detection tool that assists de-identification. It is not a certification, and its output is not expert-certified de-identified data. On documents that matter, the review step is not a courtesy — it is the control.
